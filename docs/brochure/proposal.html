AA.D.A.Always-on Domestic Automation
Pilot proposalFor Max-Migold · Draft for discussion · November 2026
Purpose

To run A.D.A. in one Max-Migold building for about six weeks, record its power and water in detail, automate the routine switching, and decide together whether and how to extend it. This is a draft to discuss on our call; every item below can be adjusted.

Scope
SiteOne building or block, chosen together: ideally one with frequent changeovers, a borehole and overhead tank, and an inverter.
DurationAbout six weeks: one week of setup, four weeks of operation, one week of review.
What's monitoredPower source and changeovers, mains voltage, generator run-hours, inverter use, tank level and pump cycles.
What's automatedBorehole pump on tank level; shedding of agreed heavy loads on inverter; cutting of sensitive loads on voltage spikes. The list of loads is agreed before installation.
HardwareLow-cost, widely available parts: an ESP32 controller board, relay module, tank level sensor, isolated voltage/source sensor, enclosure and wiring. A.D.A. provides the full parts list and configures everything. Purchase of the parts and who funds them is agreed before installation.
Out of scopeChanges to the building's generator, inverter or changeover; billing residents.
Timeline
Week 0

Agree & survey

Choose the building, walk through the distribution board and pump setup, agree loads and success measures.

Week 1

Install

Fit controller, tank sensor and hub. Mains connections by a qualified electrician. Dashboard access set up.

Weeks 2–5

Operate

A.D.A. runs and logs. Short weekly check-in and a weekly summary.

Week 6

Review

Results, findings and feedback. Decide on next steps together.

A.D.A. will
  1. Provide the hub software and dashboard for the pilot.
  2. Supply the hardware parts list and configure the controller and sensors.
  3. Coordinate installation with a qualified electrician for any mains work.
  4. Monitor the system and fix software issues during the pilot.
  5. Send a weekly summary and a final report.
Max-Migold will
  1. Choose the building and arrange access to the distribution board and pump.
  2. Obtain the hardware parts from the A.D.A. parts list, as agreed.
  3. Name one contact on site.
  4. Provide Wi-Fi access (or allow a dedicated hotspot).
  5. Share how diesel and water are handled today, so results can be compared.
  6. Give honest feedback at the weekly check-ins.

Hardware funding, installation costs and any pilot fee are to be agreed on our call and confirmed in writing before installation.

How we'll measure success
MeasureHow it's recorded
Generator hoursRun-hours per day from the changeover log, compared with the building's usual diesel use.
Avoidable generator timeHours the generator ran while the inverter still had plenty of charge.
WaterNumber of pump cycles, overflows and dry-tank events, against the previous weeks as reported by the site.
Battery protectionLowest battery level each night and how often heavy loads were shed.
Equipment protectionVoltage spikes detected and loads cut.
Team experienceFeedback from the site contact and managers: was it useful, what's missing.
What you receive

Live dashboard

For the building throughout the pilot, on phone, tablet or wall screen, with access for your team.

Weekly summary

Hours on each power source, generator and diesel estimate, pump activity, spikes, and anything unusual.

Final report

Six weeks of findings with recommendations for the building, and a proposal for next steps if it makes sense.

Risks and how they're handled
Controller or Wi-Fi failureRelays fall back to safe states; the building runs exactly as it does today. The dashboard shows "not live".
Wrong load switchedOnly loads agreed in writing are connected. Rules can be paused from the dashboard at any time.
Electrical safetyNo mains work by anyone other than a qualified electrician; installation reviewed with your site contact.
