System architecture & specification

How A.D.A. is connected, what each device measures, and how the data reaches the dashboard and the manager's phone.

1The system at a glance

230 V powersensing (isolated)low-voltage signal inside the panelnetwork (Wi-Fi / LAN)internet, optional (alerts only)

Three layers

  • Measure: isolated sensors on the supply, the inverter, the diesel tank and the water tank.
  • Decide: the hub runs the rules and keeps the log; the controller switches contactors.
  • Show & alert: the dashboard on any phone or laptop on the building network; alerts to the manager's phone.

Design principles

  • Local first: everything runs in the building. Internet is only used to push alerts.
  • Fail-safe: if the controller loses the hub for 10 s, relays drop to safe states and the building runs as it does today.
  • Mains is isolated: no 230 V reaches the controller; all mains work by a qualified electrician.

2The controller and how each device connects

Every input reaches the ESP32 at low voltage through an isolating module: optocouplers for AC presence, the PZEM's isolated interface for power, an RS485 transceiver for the inverter, a 4–20 mA current loop for the fuel probe (robust over long cable runs from the generator house) and a level-shifted echo line for the ultrasonic tank sensor. Outputs drive relay coils, and the relays drive contactor coils: the controller never carries load current.

4How the data flows

Every second

  • The controller samples each sensor, filters noise (e.g. averages the fuel probe over 30 s so sloshing is not read as theft) and sends one JSON report to the hub: {"source":"grid","readings":{"fuel_l":31.5,"battery":64,"tank":48,"voltage":228},"relays":{"pump":false}}
  • The hub runs the rules and replies with the relay states the controller must apply.
  • Dashboards poll the hub every second; if a reading is older than 5 s they say Not live and refuse to switch.

On events

  • Each change (source, switch, spike, refill, drop, delivery, offline) is written to the event log with its time and reason.
  • Alerts (diesel drop, short delivery, low diesel, low battery, low tank with no power, spike, controller offline, generator service due) are pushed to the manager's phone; once per hour per alert.
  • Per-minute totals roll up into a per-day history that builds the weekly and monthly reports.

Security and access

  • Controller must present a per-building device key.
  • People log in with PINs; roles: owner, manager, caretaker, resident. Five wrong PINs lock a device out.
  • No inbound internet access is needed; alerts go out over HTTPS.
  • Every switch is logged with who or which rule did it.

When something fails

  • Wi-Fi or hub down: relays go to safe states; dashboard shows Not live.
  • Internet down: everything works; alerts are sent when it returns, and show on the dashboard meanwhile.
  • Sensor fault: reading marked missing, not guessed; the hub keeps working on the rest.
  • Power cut: hub and router on the inverter; the controller reports the outage itself.
